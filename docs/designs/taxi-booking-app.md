## Problem Statement

The user is a taxi driver working alone (more drivers possible later). Customers book them through "a mix of everything": phone calls, WhatsApp/SMS, notes, people asking in person. No single place holds all rides, so it's hard to see when they're free, rides get forgotten, and quoting a price takes back-and-forth.

They want an app where customers log in, choose a day, time, pickup (A) and drop-off (B), see an estimated price, and request the ride if the driver is free. The driver approves each request, can change the price, and sees everything in one calendar. A back office on the web manages everything.

Reference site the user pointed to: viptransitparis.fr, a premium chauffeur service built around airport/station transfers (Orly, CDG, Le Bourget). It has a customer account, a FR/EN switch, phone and WhatsApp contact, and a vehicle choice (sedan / van up to 8). It shows **no prices** and **no availability**: every booking goes through a call or a message. Those two gaps are what this app fixes.

## What Makes This Cool

- **Price before booking.** Customer types A and B and sees an estimate in seconds. No calling to ask "how much to CDG?"
- **Real availability.** The app checks the requested time against the driver's actual day, counting travel time from the previous drop-off, not just empty clock slots.
- **One place for every ride.** Rides that still come in by phone or WhatsApp are added by the driver in a few seconds, so the calendar never lies.
- **Regulars in one tap (MVP).** A customer re-books a past ride with the same A, B, passengers and luggage; only the date and time change.

## Constraints

- One driver today; data model must support several drivers later without a rewrite.
- Customers get the app from the App Store and Google Play (user chose native store apps over a web link).
- Built with Expo (user's choice) for both iPhone and Android.
- A backend plus a web back office to "manage everything".
- Driver approves every booking request (no instant confirmation).
- Price: automatic estimate, driver can override; online payment possible but cash / card in the car must remain available.
- Customer personal data (names, phones, addresses, trips) → GDPR applies if operating in the EU.
- **Legal gate on pricing:** the price flow depends on whether the user holds a taxi licence or a VTC licence (see Pricing). Pricing work does not start until this is confirmed.

## Premises

The user called these "something to have an idea, I may be wrong also", so they are a working draft, not locked decisions.

1. The app is the one place every ride lives; phoned-in or WhatsApp rides are added manually by the driver, fast.
2. "Free" means no overlap with a ride that holds the slot once ride duration and travel time from the previous drop-off are counted.
3. The driver approves every request, can propose a different price, and the customer is notified either way.
4. Airport/station rides are first-class: flight or train number, passenger count, luggage count, meet & greet option, extra waiting time.
5. One mobile app on both stores, with a customer side and a driver side (by account role). Ready for a second driver later.
6. Online card payment is designed in but ships after booking works. Cash / card in car stays available.
7. French + English from day one.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: One Expo app + Supabase (minimal viable)
Expo app for both platforms, Supabase for login, database and live updates. Payments in phase 2. Smallest stack.

### Approach B: Full platform (ideal architecture)
Expo apps + own TypeScript API server + Postgres, availability engine, desktop dashboard, Stripe, multi-driver dispatch from the start. Much longer before a first real booking.

### Approach C: Driver cockpit first (lateral)
Build only the driver side first (quick-add, calendar, clients, price), add customer booking afterwards. Same tech as A.

### Who sees what (MVP unless marked)

- **Customer (mobile):** sign up / log in (email + password, Sign in with Apple, Google), book a ride (A → B first, then date and time, passengers, luggage, vehicle size, flight/train number, meet & greet, notes), see estimate, see status, accept or decline a proposed price, ride history, **re-book a past ride**, cancel, delete account.
- **Driver (mobile, same app, driver role):** new-request notifications with Accept / Decline / Propose price, conflict warning before accepting, today and week calendar, quick-add a ride for a phone/WhatsApp customer, block time off, mark ride done (with optional final-price adjustment, see Pricing), call / navigate to pickup. **The mobile driver side is the MVP source of truth for day-to-day work.**
- **Back office (web), MVP:** full calendar, all rides with filters, customer and contact list with history, pricing rules, working hours and time off.
- **Back office, later phases:** drivers and vehicles management, payments, accounting exports.

### Roles and access (Row Level Security)

- `customer`: reads only their own rides and profile. **No direct INSERT or UPDATE on rides**: ride creation goes through a server-side `request_ride` function that computes the estimate, `blocked_range`, initial status `requested` and validation, so the client can't set price, status or driver.
- `driver`: reads and updates rides assigned to them, plus unassigned `requested` rides; reads contacts linked to those rides.
- `admin`: reads and writes everything (back office).
- The solo owner holds both `driver` and `admin` roles. A second driver later gets `driver` only, so they never see other drivers' customers.
- Ride creation and every status change go through server-side functions only (never direct table writes from any app role), so every transition in the state machine below is checked in one place.

### Core data model

- `profiles`: auth user id, roles, name, phone, language.
- `contacts`: a customer record that does **not** need an app account: name, phone, email (optional), notes, `profile_id` (nullable), created_by, consent/notice flag. Every ride points to a contact. App customers get a contact row automatically at sign-up.
  - **Link suggestions:** when a new sign-up's **verified email** matches an existing contact, the driver/admin gets a "link this contact?" suggestion. MVP has no phone verification, so a matching **phone number** is only a weaker suggestion, labelled "phone matches (unverified)". Either way the driver/admin confirms manually. Nothing is merged automatically, because family members often share phone numbers.
  - **What linking does:** the driver-created contact survives because it holds the history. Its `profile_id` is set to the new account. Empty fields are filled from the sign-up contact, but existing driver-entered name/phone are kept. Rides from both contacts move to the surviving one, and the sign-up contact is deleted. The customer then sees all their rides in history, including phone-booked ones. Driver notes stay private to driver/admin.
- `drivers`: profile, vehicle capacity (seats, luggage), working hours, timezone, active flag. One row today.
- `rides`:
  - People and timing: contact, driver (nullable until assigned), scheduled pickup time (timestamptz).
  - Places: pickup and drop-off (address, lat/lng, Google place id).
  - Route and price: estimated distance, estimated duration, `blocked_range` (tstzrange, see Availability), estimated price, proposed price, agreed price, final price.
  - Status and history: status, status history (who, when).
  - Trip details: passengers, luggage, vehicle size requested (`sedan` / `van`), meet & greet (bool), flight/train number, notes.
  - Source and payment: source (`app`, `phone`, `whatsapp`, `in_person`, `other`), payment method + status (phase 2).
- `zones`: named areas (polygon or center + radius, or a whole city), with a type flag `airport` / `station` / `other`. Used by fixed prices and by the airport/station waiting allowance.
- `pricing_rules`: base fare, per km, per minute, minimum fare, fixed route prices (zone → zone), surcharge windows (see Pricing).
- `time_off`: driver blocked periods (timestamptz ranges).

**Capacity rule:** the booking form caps passengers and luggage at the driver's vehicle capacity. The vehicle-size option only shows sizes the driver actually has, so it's hidden while there's a single vehicle. `request_ride` validates the same limits server-side and rejects anything over capacity. Beyond the limits, the form shows "contact the driver" with call/WhatsApp buttons. Vehicle size doesn't change the price in MVP.

### Ride status state machine

Push + email go to the party named in **Notify**. "Driver" means the assigned driver, or all drivers for unassigned requests.

| From | To | Who | Notify | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (new) | `requested` | customer via `request_ride` | driver | Estimate stored as `estimated_price`. |
| (new) | `accepted` | driver/admin (quick-add) | customer, if the contact has an account | Phone/WhatsApp rides; see Quick-add validation. |
| `requested` | `accepted` | driver | customer | `agreed_price = estimated_price` (VTC). |
| `requested` | `price_proposed` | driver | customer | Driver sets `proposed_price`. Not allowed within 2 h of pickup: accept or decline instead. |
| `requested` | `declined` | driver | customer | |
| `requested` | `expired` | system | customer + driver | Deadline: 24 h after request or 2 h before pickup, whichever is first. |
| `price_proposed` | `accepted` | customer | driver | `agreed_price = proposed_price`. |
| `price_proposed` | `declined_by_customer` | customer | driver | Slot released. |
| `price_proposed` | `declined` | driver (withdraw) | customer | Driver withdraws the proposal; slot released. |
| `price_proposed` | `expired` | system | customer + driver | Deadline: 12 h after proposal or 2 h before pickup, whichever is first (Open Question 3). |
| `requested`, `price_proposed` | `cancelled` | customer | driver | Always free before acceptance. |
| `accepted` | `cancelled` | customer | driver | Fee rules are Open Question 4; MVP charges nothing. |
| `accepted` | `cancelled` | driver | customer | Reason required and shown to the customer. |
| `accepted` | `completed` | driver | customer (receipt email) | Sets `final_price` (see Pricing). |
| `accepted` | `no_show` | driver | customer | Allowed only after pickup time + the pickup allowance. |

- **Statuses that hold the slot:** `accepted` and `price_proposed`. A price proposal holds the slot so the customer's "yes" can't collide with something accepted in the meantime. Expiry and the driver's withdraw action keep it from blocking forever.
- **Not holding the slot:** `requested`, `declined`, `declined_by_customer`, `expired`, `cancelled`, `completed`, `no_show`.
- **Conflict flag:** when an acceptance or quick-add overlaps a customer's pending `requested` ride, the driver sees the flag; the customer isn't notified until the driver declines it.
- **Rides left open:** an `accepted` ride still open 2 h after its expected end triggers a driver push: "Mark as completed or no-show". Rides still open the next morning are listed on the back office home page.
- **Scheduler:** Supabase **pg_cron** runs an `expire_and_remind` function every 5 minutes. It handles expiry deadlines, open-ride reminders and retention jobs. Reads also treat a past deadline as expired, so a late cron run never shows a stale slot as taken.

### Minimum lead time

App requests must be at least **3 hours** ahead (configurable). For an earlier pickup, the app shows "Short notice: call or WhatsApp the driver" with both buttons, and the driver quick-adds the ride. The two-hour expiry deadline therefore always leaves the driver at least an hour to answer.

### Availability (first version)

**Booking flow order:** the customer enters A and B first, then picks a date and time. The app then **validates that single requested time**. It does not list all free slots, which would need a Routes API call per candidate slot.

**Check at request time** (server-side function):
1. The pickup time must fall within the driver's working hours, evaluated in the driver's timezone.
2. The new ride's own window is `[pickup, pickup + route duration + pickup allowance]`. Pickup allowance is 0 for normal rides. Airport/station pickups get a configurable waiting allowance (default 45 min, covering flight delay and baggage), and meet & greet adds 15 min. A pickup counts as airport/station when its point falls inside a zone of type `airport` or `station`, **or** a flight/train number is entered.
3. Find the nearest slot-holding ride before and after (at most 2 neighbours), plus `time_off`.
4. Call the Routes API only for those neighbours: previous drop-off → new pickup, and new drop-off → next pickup. Use the scheduled departure time for traffic-aware duration. Fallback when the API fails: straight-line distance at 30 km/h plus 15 min.
5. Free if each neighbour gap is at least **max(travel time, 15 min)** and the window doesn't overlap `time_off`.
6. If the time isn't free, suggest up to 2 alternatives, one before and one after. Candidates are taken only at neighbour boundaries: just after the previous ride's blocked end plus travel, or just before the next ride minus travel. At most 4 candidates are checked, using the **straight-line fallback estimate with no Routes calls**. When the customer picks a suggestion, it goes through the normal check in steps 1-5.

**What the database guarantees:** each ride stores `blocked_range = [pickup, pickup + route duration + pickup allowance + 15 min)`. Only the end is padded, so the database enforces a **minimum 15-minute gap** between one ride's end and the next pickup. Step 5 uses the same 15-minute floor, so the check and the constraint agree. An **exclusion constraint** on `(driver_id, blocked_range)` for slot-holding statuses makes it impossible for two slot-holding rides to overlap, even with two taps at the same moment. The travel-time buffer between two specific rides (step 4) is checked by the server-side function inside a transaction that locks the driver's row, so two acceptances can't race past it.

**Quick-add validation:** a driver's quick-add runs the same checks.
- **Outside working hours:** a warning the driver can override. It's their day.
- **Travel buffer too short:** a warning the driver can override. They may know a shortcut or the customer may be flexible.
- **Overlap with another slot-holding ride:** a hard reject by the exclusion constraint, with the message "Clashes with {customer} {time} {A → B}". The driver has to move or cancel one of the rides first.

**Competing requests:** requests don't hold the slot, so two customers can request overlapping times. When the driver accepts or proposes a price on one:
- the other overlapping `requested` rides are **flagged as conflicting** in the driver UI, and are not auto-declined;
- if the driver tries to accept a conflicting request, they see a warning naming the ride it clashes with, and the exclusion constraint blocks the acceptance;
- the driver declines or proposes another time manually. Auto-decline can be added later once real usage shows it's safe.

**Routes API cost bound:** at most 2 Routes calls per availability check, plus 1 per estimate. Suggestions use no Routes calls. Results are cached per (origin place, destination place, 15-min time bucket) for 24 h.

### Pricing (first version)

**Legal gate, confirmed before any pricing code:**
- **VTC licence:** the price is agreed in advance. The customer sees the estimate, the driver can propose a different price, and the agreed price is what the customer pays.
- **Taxi licence:** fares are regulated. The app shows the regulated fixed fare where one exists (e.g. Paris airport forfaits) and otherwise a clearly labelled **meter estimate**; the meter decides the final fare. The "propose a different price" action is then limited to what the rules allow.
- The formula below applies to the VTC case and to taxi *estimates*.

**Formula:** `max(minimum fare, base + km × per_km + minutes × per_minute)`, with distance and duration from the Routes API at the scheduled departure time.

**Fixed route prices:** a fixed price applies when both ends match a rule. Each end is either a named zone, defined as a polygon or a center + radius (e.g. "CDG terminals", "Orly"), or a whole city (e.g. "Paris intra-muros"). Fixed prices take precedence over the formula.

**Surcharges:**
- Each surcharge window is `{days, start time, end time, percent}`, in the driver's timezone.
- The **scheduled pickup time** decides whether a window applies. There's no pro-rating for rides that cross a window's start or end.
- If several windows apply (night and weekend), only the **largest** percent applies; they don't stack.
- Surcharges apply to formula prices. Whether they apply to fixed prices is a per-rule setting, off by default.
- Luggage/extra-passenger supplements are Phase 2.

**Price fields:**
- `estimated_price` is set on request.
- `proposed_price` is set when the driver proposes one.
- `agreed_price` is set on acceptance.
- **VTC:** `final_price` is set on completion and defaults to `agreed_price`. The driver may change it at completion only with a reason (extra waiting, extra stop, detour), and the customer is notified with the reason. An increase needs the customer's in-app confirmation before payment is taken (Phase 2 payments). For cash in MVP it's informational.
- **Taxi:** `agreed_price` holds the regulated fixed fare when one applies, and is empty otherwise. `final_price` is the fare entered at completion: the fixed fare, or the meter reading. No reason is required, and the "propose price" action is hidden.

### Time and timezones

All instants are stored as `timestamptz`. Working hours, `time_off` entry, surcharge windows and "day" boundaries in the calendar are evaluated in the driver's configured timezone (default `Europe/Paris`), so DST changes don't shift them. The customer app shows times in the driver's timezone, labelled.

### Privacy and data (GDPR)

- Supabase project in an EU region. Maps and email providers are listed under Dependencies & costs.
- **Retention:**
  - Completed and no-show rides are kept 10 years for accounting (French bookkeeping retention). Exact addresses on rides older than 3 years are reduced to city level.
  - Declined, expired and cancelled rides are deleted after 12 months.
  - Contacts with no completed ride and no activity for 24 months are deleted.
  - All of these run in the daily part of the pg_cron job.
- **Account deletion (store requirement):** deletes the auth account and profile, and anonymizes the linked contact (name → "Deleted customer", phone/email removed). Ride rows stay for accounting with no personal identifiers. Upcoming accepted rides are cancelled first, with confirmation.
- **Manually added contacts:** legal basis is performance of the ride the person asked for (the booking by phone). The driver's quick-add form stores a "notice given" flag. The privacy policy covers phone bookings. The person can ask for deletion, handled from the back office with the same anonymization.
- Privacy policy published on a URL for both stores.

## Success Criteria

- A customer installs the app, signs up, and requests a ride with a price estimate in under 2 minutes.
- The driver gets a notification within seconds and accepts with one tap; the customer sees "accepted".
- A ride the driver quick-adds for a phone customer (no app account) blocks that time for app customers.
- Two slot-holding rides for the same driver can never overlap: the database exclusion constraint rejects it even when both are accepted at the same moment.
- A price proposal the customer doesn't answer expires within 5 minutes of its deadline and frees the slot.
- A customer can't create a ride with a client-chosen price or status (direct inserts are rejected).
- Every ride the driver does in a week lives in the app, whatever channel it came from.

## Reviewer Concerns

Disposition: CONCERNS_RECORDED

Stop: MAX_ITERATIONS

### R3-1 — completeness

**Problem**

> The driver may propose a price up to 2 h before pickup, but a proposal expires at 2 h before pickup at the latest. A proposal made at pickup - 2 h 05 min gives the customer about 5 minutes to answer (less if the 5-minute cron runs early in that window), and it holds the slot while doing so. With the 3 h minimum lead time, this happens whenever the driver answers a short-notice request late.

**Remedy**

> Guarantee a minimum answer window: e.g. block proposals within (2 h + N min) of pickup, or give every proposal at least N minutes before it expires, and state what the driver sees when the propose action is unavailable.

### R3-2 — consistency

**Problem**

> For the taxi branch, the Pricing legal gate says the 'propose a different price' action is 'limited to what the rules allow', but Price fields says that for taxi the 'propose price' action is hidden. These two statements disagree on whether taxi drivers can propose a price at all.

**Remedy**

> Pick one rule for taxi (hidden, or allowed within stated regulatory limits) and use the same wording in both places.

### R3-3 — consistency

**Problem**

> No-show rides are kept 10 years, but contacts 'with no completed ride and no activity for 24 months' are deleted. A contact whose only rides are no-shows would be deleted while its rides must stay, which either breaks the 'every ride points to a contact' rule or cascade-deletes rides that must be kept.

**Remedy**

> Only delete contacts that have no retained rides (no completed or no_show ride), or anonymize them instead of deleting them.

### R3-4 — completeness

**Problem**

> The 24-month contact deletion does not exclude contacts linked to a live app account (profile_id set). An app customer who signed up but hasn't booked for 24 months would lose their contact row while their account stays, and every ride they create next needs a contact.

**Remedy**

> State whether contacts with a live profile are exempt, recreated on next use, or whether the inactive account is deleted too (with notice).

### R3-5 — clarity

**Problem**

> Retention lists 'Declined, expired and cancelled rides' for 12-month deletion but doesn't mention the separate status declined_by_customer, so it's unclear whether those rides are ever deleted.

**Remedy**

> List declined_by_customer explicitly in the 12-month deletion rule (or map every non-retained status to a rule).

### R3-6 — completeness

**Problem**

> Quick-add validation covers working hours, travel buffer and overlap with slot-holding rides, but not overlap with the driver's own time_off. The time_off check in Availability step 5 is not stated as a warning, a hard block, or skipped for quick-add.

**Remedy**

> State the quick-add behavior for a time_off overlap (e.g. a warning the driver can override, since it's their own block).

### R3-7 — consistency

**Problem**

> Account deletion says ride rows 'stay for accounting with no personal identifiers', but Retention keeps exact pickup/drop-off addresses (often a home address, which is personal data) for up to 3 years. The two statements contradict each other.

**Remedy**

> On account deletion, either reduce ride addresses to city level at once, or change the wording and document the legal basis for keeping exact addresses until the 3-year reduction.

### R3-8 — completeness

**Problem**

> Account deletion cancels upcoming accepted rides first, but says nothing about the customer's pending requested or price_proposed rides. A price_proposed ride holds the slot and would stay attached to an anonymized contact.

**Remedy**

> State that account deletion also cancels pending requested/price_proposed rides (releasing the slot and notifying the driver).
